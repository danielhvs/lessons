Fractions

How to write, compare, add, multiply and divide pieces of one whole.

1The problem

One chocolate bar for 3 people: how much does each get? Not 1 bar, not 0 bars. Whole numbers (0, 1, 2, 3...) have no answer for this. A fraction is the number that counts pieces of one whole cut into equal parts: each person gets 1/3 of the bar.

2The smallest example: 3/4

Cut a bar into 4 equal pieces and take 3. That is 3/4 (three quarters).

The denominator is the bottom number: how many equal pieces the whole is cut into (4). The numerator is the top number: how many pieces you take (3). Each piece is 1/4, and 1 piece is left over.

3Same amount, different names

Shade half a bar: 1/2. Cut every piece in 2: now there are 4 pieces and 2 shaded, 2/4. Cut every piece in 3 instead: 3/6. The shaded part did not change size.

These are equivalent fractions: different names for the same amount. The rule: multiply the numerator and the denominator by the same number.

1/2 = (1×2)/(2×2) = 2/4
1/2 = (1×3)/(2×3) = 3/6
python3 -c "from fractions import Fraction as F; print(F(1, 2) == F(2, 4) == F(3, 6))" True

4Simplify

To simplify is the way back: divide top and bottom by the same number, until you reach the smallest numbers that give the same amount.

In 6/8, the biggest number that divides both 6 and 8 is 2. That number is the GCD (greatest common divisor). Divide both by 2:

6/8 = (6÷2)/(8÷2) = 3/4
python3 -c "from fractions import Fraction as F; print(F(6, 8))" 3/4

5Compare: which is bigger?

2/3 or 3/5? The pieces are different sizes (thirds and fifths), so comparing 2 with 3 says nothing.

Cut both bars into the same size pieces. 15 works, because 3 × 5 = 15:

2/3 = (2×5)/(3×5) = 10/15
3/5 = (3×3)/(5×3) = 9/15

Now just count: 10 is more than 9, so 2/3 is bigger, by 1/15. That shared bottom (15) is called a common denominator.

python3 -c "from fractions import Fraction as F; print(F(2, 3) > F(3, 5), F(2, 3) - F(3, 5))" True 1/15

6Add

With the same denominator, add the numerators and keep the denominator, because the piece size did not change:

1/5 + 2/5 = 3/5

With different denominators, first rewrite both fractions with a common denominator. The smallest one is the LCM (least common multiple): the smallest number both denominators divide into. The LCM of 2 and 3 is 6:

1/2 + 1/3
= 3/6 + 2/6
= 5/6
python3 -c "from fractions import Fraction as F; print(F(1, 2) + F(1, 3))" 5/6

7Multiply: a fraction of a fraction

2/3 × 3/4 means "2/3 of 3/4". Shade 3 of 4 columns (3/4), and inside them take 2 of 3 rows (2/3). The purple cells are in both parts.

Rule: multiply top by top and bottom by bottom.

2/3 × 3/4 = (2×3)/(3×4) = 6/12 = 1/2
python3 -c "from fractions import Fraction as F; print(F(2, 3) * F(3, 4))" 1/2

8Divide: how many fit?

3 ÷ 1/2 asks: how many halves fit in 3 wholes? Each whole holds 2 halves, so 6.

Shortcut: dividing by a fraction is multiplying by its reciprocal, the fraction flipped upside down. The reciprocal of 1/2 is 2/1:

3 ÷ 1/2 = 3 × 2/1 = 6
python3 -c "from fractions import Fraction as F; print(3 / F(1, 2))" 6

9More than one whole

In 7/4 the numerator is bigger than the denominator: it is more than one whole. That is an improper fraction.

4 quarters fill one bar and 3 are left: 7/4 = 1 whole and 3/4. Written as a mixed number: 1 3/4, which is 1.75 as a decimal.

python3 -c "from fractions import Fraction as F; print(F(7, 4), float(F(7, 4)))" 7/4 1.75

10Common mistakes

Adding tops and bottoms: 1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5 is wrong. 2/5 = 0.4 is smaller than 1/2 = 0.5, and adding cannot make it smaller. Fix: common denominator first, 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.

python3 -c "from fractions import Fraction as F; print(F(1, 2) + F(1, 3) == F(2, 5), float(F(2, 5)))" False 0.4

Thinking a bigger denominator means a bigger fraction: 1/4 is smaller than 1/3. Cutting into more pieces makes each piece smaller.

Changing only one number: 1/2 → 2/2 is not equivalent (2/2 is one whole). To keep the same amount, multiply or divide top and bottom by the same number.

Flipping the wrong fraction when dividing: in 3 ÷ 1/2, the one that flips is the one after the sign (1/2 → 2/1), not the 3.

Summary

  1. Denominator (bottom): how many equal pieces the whole is cut into. Numerator (top): how many you take.
  2. Multiplying or dividing top and bottom by the same number keeps the same amount (equivalent fractions, simplify).
  3. To compare, add or subtract, first make the pieces the same size (common denominator, the LCM).
  4. Multiply: top × top, bottom × bottom. Divide: multiply by the reciprocal.
  5. A numerator bigger than the denominator is more than one whole: 7/4 = 1 3/4.
